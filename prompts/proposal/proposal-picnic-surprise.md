# Picnic Surprise

## The prompt (copy-paste)

> A couple sits on a picnic blanket in a sunny park, a basket and glasses between them. He reaches into the basket and pulls out a small ring box instead of the bottle, holding it up with a nervous smile. She freezes mid-laugh, eyes widening, one hand flying to her mouth. Dappled sunlight moves gently through the trees above them. Locked-off static camera. Sweet, sunny, candid, documentary feel.

## Negative prompt

> ring box multiplying, hands morphing, basket tipping over, glasses spilling, faces swapping, sunlight strobing

## Formula used

Wan image-to-video: **one surprise beat, locked-off camera**. The whole
shot is a freeze-frame reaction — Wan holds a still scene faithfully and
renders one small motion (the hand to the mouth) cleanly.

## Settings

- Mode: image-to-video (Wan 2.2)
- Duration: 5 seconds
- Aspect ratio: 16:9

## Tips

- Five seconds is the surprise and the reaction. Nothing else needs to
  happen.
- Dappled light is risky — if the shadows strobe, shoot the still in open
  shade instead and let the model add the light.
- This is the "she said yes" film's opening beat: casual, sunny, then the
  question lands.
