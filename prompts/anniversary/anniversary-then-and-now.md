# Then and Now

## The prompt (copy-paste)

> A couple holds up their old wedding photograph in front of them, the young couple in the print aligned with the older couple holding it, faces peeking over the top edge of the print. The photograph stays steady in their hands; the older couple smiles gently over it. The camera pushes in very slowly on the photograph. Tender, clever, warm daylight, a single image holding fifty years.

## Negative prompt

> photograph bending or flapping, hands morphing, faces in the print changing, the print sliding away, camera shaking

## Formula used

Wan image-to-video: **almost no motion** — a steady hold and a very slow
push-in. The idea is the composition, not the movement. Wan holds a still
composition faithfully, which is exactly what this shot needs.

## Settings

- Mode: image-to-video (Wan 2.2)
- Duration: 5 seconds
- Aspect ratio: 16:9
- Keep it at 5 seconds: the joke lands fast, and longer invites drift

## Tips

- Line the photo up before you shoot the still: the young faces in the
  print should sit just below the older faces holding it.
- Shoot in even daylight so the print has no glare — glare becomes a
  moving smear in the video.
- Use this as the final shot of a then-and-now sequence, or as the
  thumbnail frame for the whole film.
